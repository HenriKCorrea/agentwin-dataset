\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

This chapter defines the cross-domain use case used to demonstrate AgenTwin,
    followed by the evaluation methodology that assesses the artifact's
    semantic, functional, and performance characteristics.
The use case provides the concrete operational scenario against which all evaluation
    dimensions are measured, grounding the assessment in a realistic deployment context.

\section{Cross-Domain Use Case Definition}
\label{sec:use-case}

To validate AgenTwin's agent-mediated interoperability approach,
    a realistic cross-domain scenario has been designed that requires
    cooperation between Smart City and Energy Grid DT systems.

The use case addresses the operational challenge of coordinating overnight EV fleet charging
    across facilities managed by two organizationally separate institutions:
    the New York City Department of Citywide Administrative Services (DCAS),
    which operates the city's fleet EV charging network across municipal garages in all five boroughs,
    and Consolidated Edison (ConEdison),
    the distribution utility responsible for the electrical grid serving those facilities.
Depot charging is a centralized EV charging model in which fleet vehicles return to
    a common operating facility for scheduled energy replenishment during non-operational hours,
    enabling predictable vehicle availability and simplified infrastructure
    management.
The challenge intensifies for mixed fleets because power requirements vary substantially
    by vehicle category: light-duty vehicles typically charge at 7--22~kW,
    while heavy-duty vehicles may require over 120~kW,
    making uniform infrastructure planning insufficient.
Moreover, when the combined electrical demand of a fleet approaches a facility's
    service capacity, grid upgrades may be required to prevent overloading
    the distribution infrastructure~\cite{monta2026}.
In an operational context, this means that a safe overnight charging assignment
    cannot be derived from charging station data alone:
    it requires real-time knowledge of transformer headroom held exclusively
    by the serving utility.

\textbf{User Persona:}
A Fleet Coordinator at DCAS is responsible for ensuring that vehicles across several
    city departments are road-ready at morning dispatch.
Each night, as vehicles return to their respective agency facilities across the five boroughs,
    the coordinator must confirm that the combined overnight charging load can be accommodated
    by the local distribution grid at each facility without exceeding transformer capacity.
DCAS operates charging infrastructure for a heterogeneous fleet spanning five agency categories,
    each corresponding to a distinct \texttt{allowedVehicleType} in the NYC Fleet Station Network:
    NYPD patrol and detective sedans (\texttt{car}:
        Chevrolet Bolt EV at 7.2~kW, Hyundai Ioniq~5 at 10.9~kW);
    FDNY Emergency Medical Services paramedic response units (\texttt{car}:
        Hyundai Ioniq~5);
    DSNY refuse collection trucks (\texttt{truck}:
        Mack~LR Electric at up to 150~kW DC);
    DOE electric school buses (\texttt{bus}:
        Thomas Built Buses Jouley at up to 60~kW DC);
    and a NYCEM mobile command vehicle (\texttt{caravan}:
        Mercedes-Benz eSprinter EV at 22~kW)~\cite{dcas2026cftp}.
Power requirements span more than an order of magnitude across these categories,
    making uniform infrastructure planning insufficient.
The coordinator opens AgenTwin and submits a natural-language request:
    \textit{``I need to schedule tonight's depot charge.
    Incoming vehicles: 8 Chevrolet Bolt EVs (NYPD), 4 Hyundai Ioniq~5s (FDNY EMS),
    2 Thomas Built Jouley school buses (DOE), 1 eSprinter mobile command unit (NYCEM),
    and 2 Mack~LR Electric collection trucks (DSNY).
    Which stations can charge all of them without exceeding grid limits?''}

\textbf{Cross-Domain Requirement:}
This request crosses an organizational data sovereignty boundary:
    DCAS and ConEdison are independent institutions operating separate Digital Twin systems
    over the same physical infrastructure,
    with no shared data model:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{Smart City DT} (DCAS) knows charging station locations,
        plug counts, operational status, and current slot availability,
        but has no access to ConEdison's transformer loads or grid capacity data
    \item The \textbf{Energy Grid DT} (ConEdison) monitors distribution transformer loads
        and computes available headroom per substation zone,
        but does not model DCAS charging station infrastructure or fleet vehicle assignments
\end{itemize}

AgenTwin bridges these two platforms:
    it correlates charging station locations with ConEdison substation service zones,
    computes available capacity per transformer zone,
    and produces a fleet-wide charging assignment that satisfies both
    station availability and the grid's maximum import constraint.

\textbf{Interaction Workflow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator submits request} via natural language through user interface,
        identifying vehicle models and fleet size.
    \item \textbf{Vehicle enrichment:} the Energy Grid agent resolves each vehicle model against
        \texttt{ElectricVehicle} entities in Ditto,
        retrieving \texttt{chargeRateMax} per vehicle model without requiring
        the operator to provide electrical specifications.
    \item \textbf{Infrastructure query:} the Smart City agent queries \texttt{EVChargingStation}
        entities in KTWIN, filtering by
        \texttt{availableCapacity > 0} and
        \texttt{status = working}.
    \item \textbf{Grid capacity query:} the Energy Grid agent aggregates
        \texttt{CustomerLoad.p} and \texttt{EquivalentLoad.p} values
        per ConEdison substation zone in Ditto,
        computing available headroom as
        $\texttt{headroom} = \texttt{ratedS} - \Sigma(\texttt{CustomerLoad.p} + \texttt{EquivalentLoad.p})$.
    \item \textbf{Geographic--topological bridge:} the agents correlate each candidate station's
        GeoJSON coordinates (\texttt{GeoLocation}) with the CIM \texttt{Location} position points
        of ConEdison substation zones to determine which substation serves each station.
        This step constitutes the primary semantic gap: the two platforms
        represent geographic position in structurally incompatible formats with no shared identifier.
    \item \textbf{Assignment computation:} each vehicle is mapped to a station whose substation zone
        can absorb its \texttt{chargeRateMax} while maintaining at least 5\% safety headroom.
        Vehicles with higher power requirements are preferentially assigned to zones
        with greater available capacity.
    \item \textbf{Assignment proposal presented} to the operator as a structured natural-language
        response listing each vehicle and its assigned charging station.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Evaluation Scenario:}
Both DT platforms are pre-populated with a known city state drawn from real-world sources:
    ConEdison substation locations sourced from OpenStreetMap,
    and DCAS fleet charging station locations from the NYC Open Data portal,
    with transformer electrical parameters synthesized from representative overnight baseline loads.
AgenTwin is designed with no manually coded mapping between the two platforms:
    all semantic bridging is performed by the domain agents at runtime
    through MCP queries and A2A negotiation.
    The evaluation verifies that this architectural property produces
    a complete, constraint-satisfying assignment.

\textbf{Success Criteria:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item All vehicles in the request receive a valid charging station assignment
    \item No ConEdison transformer zone exceeds 95\% of rated capacity after the full assignment
    \item Only stations with \texttt{status = working} and \texttt{availableCapacity > 0} are assigned
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Semantic Gap Characterization}
\label{subsec:semantic-gaps}

The scenario exposes three distinct semantic gaps between the
    \texttt{smart-city} ontology (KTWIN) and the
    \texttt{energy-grid} ontology (Ditto).
These gaps span three categories of semantic heterogeneity:
    \emph{conceptual} (same physical object modeled from incompatible domain perspectives),
    \emph{structural} (same geographic concept encoded in incompatible data formats), and
    \emph{vocabulary} (a concept present in neither ontology that the agent must resolve
    from parametric world knowledge).
Figure~\ref{fig:patched-semantic-gap} illustrates the cross-domain ontology model
    after the \texttt{PowerSystemResource.Location} patch described in
    Section~\ref{sec:ontology-preparation},
    annotating each entity by its role in the coordination decision,
    and Table~\ref{tab:semantic-gaps} summarizes the three identified gaps.
Entities marked \texttt{<<key>>} are directly read or compared during the assignment decision;
    \texttt{<<bridge>>} entities mark cross-domain semantic gaps the agent must negotiate at runtime;
    \texttt{<<context>>} entities provide structural topology for graph traversal;
    \texttt{<<open-reference>>} marks a concept referenced by one ontology but defined in the other.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:evaluation-methodology}

The evaluation assesses whether AgenTwin achieves its design goal:
    enabling agent-mediated semantic interoperability between heterogeneous Digital Twin systems
    without requiring manually coded integration between platforms.
Following the DSR evaluation principle of demonstrating artifact feasibility,
    the scope is deliberately bounded to the fleet charging coordination use case.

The evaluation is structured as three layered dimensions that build on one another.
Semantic mapping evaluation establishes, at the component level, that agents correctly
    bridge each identified semantic gap before any end-to-end claim is made.
Functional evaluation then verifies, end-to-end, that the artifact produces correct
    outputs across nominal and boundary operating conditions.
Performance evaluation characterises the operational cost of the integration mechanism,
    informing whether the approach is practical for deployment.

Following the DSR generalizability argument, the selected scenario is canonical:
    it exercises all three categories of semantic heterogeneity identified as
    constitutive of the problem class (conceptual, structural, vocabulary).
Any additional scenario would add instances of already-demonstrated categories
    rather than exposing new gap types.

\subsection{Semantic Mapping Evaluation}
\label{subsec:semantic-mapping-evaluation}

Semantic mapping evaluation assesses whether agents correctly bridge each semantic gap
    identified in Table~\ref{tab:semantic-gaps}.
Correctness is verified through programmatic assertions on A2A task completion artifacts
    produced at the end of each test scenario.
The artifact provides three neutral output fields: \texttt{vehicle\_id},
    \texttt{station\_id}, and \texttt{zone\_id} that encode the result
    without prescribing how it was produced.
Each field serves as an independent evaluation oracle for its corresponding gap:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conceptual Gap}
        (\texttt{EVChargingStation} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{CustomerLoad}):
    For each assignment in the artifact, the test harness independently verifies
        that the assigned \texttt{station\_id}'s WGS84 coordinates
        fall within the geographic boundary of the reported \texttt{zone\_id}.
    A zone attribution error---possible only if the agent failed to correlate
        charging station locations with substation service areas---produces
        a station-zone mismatch that fails this assertion.

    \item \textbf{Structural Gap}
        (\texttt{GeoLocation} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{Location}/\texttt{CoordinateSystem}):
    The test harness independently applies the CRS transformation from the
        \texttt{crsUrn} declared in the Energy Grid DT
        to the substation's \texttt{PositionPoints},
        and verifies that the resulting WGS84 boundary is consistent with
        the \texttt{zone\_id} reported in the artifact.
    A CRS resolution error produces a geographic misclassification
        detectable by comparing the independently-transformed coordinates
        against zone boundaries.

    \item \textbf{Vocabulary Gap}
        (\texttt{allowedVehicleType} and \texttt{evName}):
    The test harness maintains a ground-truth vehicle model-to-type table
        as test infrastructure.
    For each assignment, it verifies that the \texttt{station\_id}'s
        \texttt{allowedVehicleType} includes the ground-truth type
        for the assigned \texttt{vehicle\_id}'s model name.
    A type inference error produces a vehicle-station type incompatibility
        that fails this assertion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Evaluation}
\label{subsec:functional-evaluation}

Functional evaluation verifies that the artifact produces correct outputs
    across several test scenarios, each targeting a distinct constraint dimension.
Each scenario is submitted as an A2A task to AgenTwin and evaluated once
    in a controlled, reproducible environment.
Significant failures in multi-agent pipelines arise from structural coupling between
    agents, tools, and shared context rather than from isolated stochastic draws~\cite{wegener2026};
    such failures are therefore consistent across executions and detectable in a single run.
Consistent with the DSR evaluation goal of demonstrating feasibility,
    a scenario passes if the observed artifact fields match the expected values;
    a single counterexample is sufficient to refute a capability claim.

Following the behavioral testing principle of black-box evaluation~\cite{ribeiro2020},
    success criteria are defined over observable output fields
    rather than internal agent reasoning traces.
Inspecting reasoning traces introduces a white-box paradox:
    an agent could emit a correct answer through a spurious reasoning,
    or produce a correct reasoning chain yet fail to complete the task.
The scenarios are therefore designed as \emph{Minimum Functionality Tests} (MFTs):
    each targets one specific capability of the system,
    and is constructed so that a plausible wrong-mechanism strategy---the natural shortcut
    an agent would take if it had not correctly bridged the gap---produces
    a \emph{systematically different} observable output value.
AgenTwin is an agentic multi-system pipeline rather than a single NLP model,
    so the capability taxonomy from~\cite{ribeiro2020} does not transfer directly;
    the black-box MFT principle is applied at the level of cross-domain semantic gap resolution.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nominal Scenario:}
    Execute the cross-domain use case described in Section~\ref{sec:use-case}
        with transformer headroom sufficient for the full vehicle fleet.
    Verifies all three semantic gap assertions:
        every \texttt{station\_id} must be geographically consistent with its \texttt{zone\_id},
        CRS transformation must yield the correct zone attribution,
        and each vehicle-station pair must satisfy \texttt{allowedVehicleType} compatibility.
    Success requires all 17 vehicles assigned,
        \texttt{unresolvable\_vehicles} empty, and \texttt{oversubscription} null.

    \item \textbf{Oversubscription Scenario:}
    Reduce available transformer headroom in the platform data so that it
        cannot accommodate the full fleet,
        keeping the operator request unchanged.
    Verifies that the system identifies the capacity constraint through platform queries,
        assigns vehicles in zones with sufficient headroom,
        and moves vehicles in the oversubscribed zone to
        \texttt{unresolvable\_vehicles} with reason: \texttt{no\_capacity}.
    Success requires \texttt{oversubscription} non-null with the correct zone
        and shortfall values, and no vehicle assigned to a zone exceeding
        95\% of rated capacity.

    \item \textbf{Unknown Vehicle Scenario:}
    Extend the nominal fleet with one vehicle whose model name is absent
        from both ontologies and the agent's training knowledge
        (\textit{ElectraMax X7}),
        with transformer headroom unchanged from the nominal scenario.
    Verifies the vocabulary gap negative case:
        the agent must not silently assign an unknown vehicle type to a station.
    Success requires the unknown vehicle to appear in \texttt{unresolvable\_vehicles}
        with reason: \texttt{type\_inference\_failed}
        and to be absent from \texttt{assignments}.

    \item \textbf{Type-Incompatible Station Scenario:}
    Configure the platform data so that all charging stations across the deployment
        exclusively accept \texttt{allowedVehicleType: car},
        then include one vehicle in the fleet whose model name resolves to
        \texttt{truck} via the ground-truth type table.
    Verifies that the agent enforces type compatibility for known vehicle types:
        the agent must not silently assign the truck to a car-only station.
    Success requires the truck vehicle to appear in \texttt{unresolvable\_vehicles}
        with reason: \texttt{no\_compatible\_station}
        and to be absent from \texttt{assignments}.

    \item \textbf{Topological Attribution Scenario:}
    Configure the test data with one charging station $C_1$
        whose WGS84 coordinates place it geographically closer to substation $S_1$
        than to substation $S_2$,
        but whose corresponding \texttt{CustomerLoad} in the Energy Grid DT is
        connected to $S_2$ via the CIM graph path
        \texttt{CustomerLoad} $\to$ \texttt{Terminal} $\to$
        \texttt{ConnectivityNode} $\to$ \texttt{Terminal} $\to$
        \texttt{PowerTransformerEnd} $\to$ \texttt{Substation}.
    Because power physically flows through $S_2$'s transformer,
        capacity headroom must be debited from $S_2$, not $S_1$.
    Verifies that the agent traverses the ontology graph to determine zone attribution
        rather than using geographic proximity as a surrogate.
    The test harness maintains a topological ground-truth table
        derived by following the CIM graph path for each station.
    Success requires the \texttt{zone\_id} reported for the $C_1$'s assignment
        to match the CIM-derived zone ($S_2$'s service area),
        not the geographically nearest zone ($S_1$'s service area);
        a proximity-based attribution produces \texttt{zone\_id} $= S_1$,
        which fails this assertion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Evaluation}
\label{subsec:performance-evaluation}

Performance evaluation characterizes the operational overhead incurred by
    the agent-mediated integration mechanism.
Because AgenTwin is a novel approach with no prior implementation to compare against,
    no external baseline and no numeric acceptance threshold can be derived from
    existing literature or practice at this stage of the research.
The goal is to produce empirical data that supports a qualitative feasibility judgement
    and establishes a reference point for subsequent projects.
The results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:performance-results}
    and interpreted in the Discussion (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task Completion Time:} % Suggestion: use LangFuse
    Measure total time from operator submission to delivery of the complete assignment proposal,
        including any clarification turns between the agent and the operator.
    Report mean, median, and 95th percentile times across multiple executions.

    \item \textbf{LLM Resource Consumption:} % Suggestion: use LangFuse
    Record the number of LLM inference calls and total tokens consumed
        per assignment request.
    These metrics constitute the dominant cost driver in agent-mediated systems
        and determine infrastructure requirements for realistic deployment.
\end{itemize}
